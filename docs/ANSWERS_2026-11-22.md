## The 1186 charter (decided 23 Nov 2026: keep the date)

David: "keep the date". The book prints 7 March 1186 as the first written mention, as before; the note keeps the indiction and Mayer's verdict for the reader.

The charter that first names Ramallah ("by which one goes to Ramallah") has two problems.
1. **The date.** It reads "mclxxxvi, indiction v". Indiction V is 1187, so "7 March 1186" is probably 7 March **1187** in modern reckoning. Guy was king by then, so "rex" is right and Strehlke's objection falls away.
2. **Authenticity.** Hans Eberhard Mayer, the editor of the royal charters of Jerusalem (MGH, 2010), classes it as a **forgery**, and the Revised Regesta (RRR no. 1167) follows him. Mayer is the same scholar the book already follows in rejecting the "1198" Marseilles document.

The book now says both things in the note, and the headline sentence reads: "The name first appears in a charter dated 1186, not in 1279; if Mayer is right that the charter is forged, the earliest sure date is the thirteenth century, and still before 1279." **David to decide** whether "1186" should stay as the first-mention date elsewhere in the book (the timeline, chapter 6's heading, the film), or be printed as "1186 (a charter Mayer holds to be forged)". Mayer's full argument is in MGH *DD Jerus.* ii, 793–95 (no. 472), which needs a library.
