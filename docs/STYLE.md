# The voice of *The Roots of Ramallah* (v106)

One voice from the first page to the last. It gathers the rules the book already follows: v64 "One voice", v78 "Smooth everything", v83, and David's notes of November 2026. Where the book used two forms, the more common form in the book's own prose is the rule below.

## 1. Who is speaking
- **We, the family.** The book speaks as "we" and "our family", to the family and its children: "our town", "our grandparents", "we print both". It never says "I" and never says "the author". (In quotations, the speaker keeps their own words.)
- **Warm and plain.** Short sentences, one idea each. Ordinary words before technical ones. A term is explained the first time it appears: *madāfa*, the clan guest house.
- **Calm about violence.** State what happened, who did it, where and when, with the source, and let the facts carry the weight. No adjectives of outrage ("brutal", "horrific", "heroic"), and no slogans. A judgement is printed only when it is someone's, and named as theirs ("the UN's Commission of Inquiry concluded…").
- **Honest about evidence.** Say how we know: documented, remembered, inferred. An inference is labelled as ours. A gap is printed as a gap.
- **The book does not talk about itself.** It never mentions earlier editions, drafts, versions or corrections ("as printed in earlier editions", "this edition puts its weight", "now corrected"). It states what is true. Disagreements between named sources are printed both ways, by name. (David, v78.)
- **No small-discrepancy notes.** Off-by-a-few totals and spelling variants between sources are not remarked on. Real conflicts of date, place or person are kept.
- **Readers' invitations** close a chapter in one fixed form: "If you know of …, we would like to hear from you." Speaking to the reader as "you" is otherwise kept to the young readers' pages ("Steadfast", the "What was happening" boxes).

## 2. Structure
- Each story chapter opens with what was happening and ends with "What we still don't know".
- Entry titles are short and in sentence case.
- Each paragraph leads with its point. Bold lead-ins ("**The curfews.**") are used for paragraph headings inside long entries, and end with a full stop.

## 3. Names and places
- **Places** are spelled as the book's maps spell them, in its transliteration: al-Bīra, Bayt Jālā, Baytūniyā, Qalandiyā, al-Jalazūn, al-Amʿarī, al-Manāra, Jifnā, Karak, Maʿīn, Ghassān, the Ḥaddādīn. Ramallah, Jerusalem, Nablus, Hebron, Jaffa, Acre, Lydda, Ramle, Gaza, Amman and Madaba keep their usual English forms. Ṭaybeh is the village the Gospel calls Ephraim (not al-Ṭayyiba).
- **Institutions keep their own English names**: Birzeit University (and Birzeit, the town), the Ramallah Friends School, the Muqāṭaʿa. Settlements are named as they are generally known in English: Beit El, Psagot, Ofra.
- **People**: Naseeb Shaheen (author, 1992 and 2005) and ʿAzīz Shāhīn (author, 1982) are two people, and their two spellings are kept apart. Joseph (Yūsuf) Cadora, of the Saah clan.
- **Quotations, titles of works, citations and file names are never respelled.**

## 4. Numbers, dates, words
- Dates: 6 June 1967. Decades: the 1930s. Centuries in words: the sixteenth century.
- Numbers: one to ten in words in prose, 11 and up in figures, except at the start of a sentence; ages and counts of people in prose may be words when short ("sixty-nine people"). Use "per cent" in prose, % in tables and charts.
- British spelling: organisation, centre, neighbour, recognise.
- Curly quotation marks, “double” outside and ‘single’ inside. Spaced em dashes — like this — and en dashes for ranges (1936–1939).
- Wars and periods: the first intifada, the second intifada (lower case, as the book has them), the Nakba, the Great Revolt, the June war of 1967, the Mandate.

## 5. Sources (unchanged)
Palestinian and Arab sources decide; Israeli authors are context only. Every claim carries its source in the notes. See `docs/METHOD.md` and `docs/MAP_STYLE.md`.
