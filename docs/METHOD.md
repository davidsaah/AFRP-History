# Method

## 1. The chart is a guide, not a source

John Mogannam's chart of thirty-six named generations is the document this project
worked from for years. Earlier editions of the book were *built on it*. That was
the wrong way round.

Its compiler is one of this book's two authors, which makes the point sharper
rather than softer: **a chart cannot be its own evidence, least of all when the
people printing it are the people who drew it.** So it is set out whole in an
appendix as the family's own guide — and a very good one — with the points at
which the texts correct it marked, and everything in the chain is built from
Genesis, the Arabic genealogists, Procopius and the registers directly.

Every correction to that chart in this book is a correction the co-authors make to
themselves.

## 2. Palestinian and Arab scholarship first

**Eastern sources are cited as the source; Western editors are cited as the
route.** Ibn Durayd's etymology is Ibn Durayd's, not a modern Western reading of
it. The Jabal Usays inscription was first read by Muḥammad Abū al-Faraj al-ʿUsh.
The 1596 register was tabulated by Kamal Abdulfattah of Birzeit with Hütteroth.
Where a claim reaches us through a Western scholar, both are named and the order
says which is which.

Moshe Sharon does not carry the timeline anywhere in this book.

## 3. Print uncertainty rather than smooth it

Corrections appear in marked boxes at the date where they happen, **at the same
size as the claim they replace**, with the superseded reading quoted. Retractions
of this book's own findings are printed the same way — including the retraction of
a false trail that two earlier editions published.

Grades are printed on every entry: `scripture · classical · attested · documents ·
oral tradition · no evidence`. A **TWO WITNESSES** badge means two genuinely
independent texts carry the claim; **ONE TRADITION** means both carry it but are
drinking from the same well. The distinction was introduced after research showed
that the family's 2026 presentation reproduces a modern reconstruction rather than
an independent source.

## 4. Cite everything

Author, work, year, page, DOI or URL. Where a negative check has been run —
"searched in full and it is not there" — the book says which edition was searched
and that the silence is weak evidence.

## 5. The story is Ramallah's people

Where the same material can be told through the townspeople and the villagers of
the district or through kings and battles, it is told through the people. The
missions came to Ramallah because Ramallah's people asked them for things and then
ran what they were given. A waqf is not a legal category; it is a tithe, and a
tithe means a crop, and a crop means hands.

## 6. How the generations are numbered

Two numbers appear in the book and they are not the same.

- **The chart's number** counts its own thirty-six rows, one of which is a
  nine-hundred-year void drawn as a single line.
- **This book's number** counts generations: **sixty-six** from Adam and Eve to
  Rāshid, and about eighteen more to this morning — **eighty-four in all**.

Generations 1 to 34 carry the same number in both. So *"the Mogannam chart,
generation 36"* means Rāshid's row on the chart, and *"generation 66"* means
Rāshid.

The arithmetic: al-Ḥārith b. Jabala died 569; Rāshid flourished about 1500. That
is **931 years**, which at the observed male-line interval of 25–35 years is
**27 to 37 generations, most probably 31**. The chart draws two. Forty-five — the
chart's own label — would require a generation of twenty-one years, which no
observed population sustains.
