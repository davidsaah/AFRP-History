## What changed in the book

### Chapter 10 — the void

**New · GHASSĀN IS STILL THERE IN 1400.** The documentary void is not six hundred years. Ghassān is entered as a living people with three addresses — al-Balqāʾ, the Yarmūk, Ḥimṣ — in al-Qalqashandī's *Qalāʾid al-jumān* (ed. Ibyārī, p. 97), sourced through al-ʿUmarī's *Masālik al-abṣār* to **Badr al-Dīn Yūsuf b. Zammākh al-Ḥamdānī, *mihmandār al-ʿarab*** — the Mamluk officer paid to know which tribes existed and where they camped. Prints the transmission chain end to end, and three cautions: the locations are east and north, al-Qalqashandī is copying not observing, and he changed one word.

**New · THE WORD UNDERNEATH.** The word he changed. Al-ʿUmarī files these Ghassānids under ***ṣalība*** — pure, direct descent — and says they "have passed out of the reckoning of Arabs and become townspeople" (*Masālik* iv.358). That is the mechanism of the silence, stated in the fourteenth century by the man compiling the list. **And the same section names Wādī Banī Zayd** — this district — with two settled lineages in it, a hundred and sixty years before Rāshid.

**New · AND THE MAN WHO SHOULD HAVE KNOWN.** Mujīr al-Dīn, Jerusalem 1495, in Rāshid's own generation. Printed at full strength against the above, with its own weakness declared.

**New · THE TRADITION HAS ITS OWN WORD FOR THIS.** ***Nasab al-qaṭʿ***, defined by Ibn ʿInaba (*ʿUmdat al-ṭālib*, p. 372) and applied by him at p. 161: where the chain is cut, "whoever traces his descent to them needs additional clarity in his proof." With Ibn Khaldūn naming Ghassān among the mixed lineages at *Muqaddima* II.9. The family is not failing a modern test — it is failing, and admitting, a fourteenth-century one.

**New · WHAT THE OTHER ARCHIVE CALLS THEM.** Syriac says *Ṭayyāyē* and *Bēth Ḥārith*; Millar: no source from inside the Roman empire, in any of its four languages, contains an expression translatable as "Ghassanid" or "Jafnid."

**New · FIVE SEARCHES AND WHAT THEY ACTUALLY PROVE.** The Shāmila name-test printed with its controls visible, so the reader can see that Dayr Ghassāna returning zero is a fact about the library.

**New · THE INTERVAL NOBODY MEASURED FOR US.** The generation interval as a stated assumption; J-Z640 as a claim that fails on inspection; the Palestinian-genetics gap.

### Chapter 11 — Karak

**New · KARAK WAS A CHRISTIAN TOWN.** 1596: 103 Christian households against 78 Muslim — 57% of the taxable households. **And Burckhardt's sentence that runs the family's road backwards**: the Karak Christians are "descendants of refugees from Jerusalem, Bethlehem, and Beit Djade."

**New · MUSIL ASKED THEM HIMSELF.** Arabia Petraea III, 84–85 — he went round the lineages and asked each one. The Ḥaddādīn named **Khirbet al-Ḥaddād**, a ruin on their own plateau in sight of their own castle. All eight Christian clans give non-Palestinian origins. **Four accounts now, printed as four.**

## Claims withdrawn before print

These were in my synthesis of 14 September. Verification killed them.

1. **The Chabot footnote — withdrawn entirely.** Chabot's *Michel le Syrien* searched in full: "Ghassan" and derivatives occur **zero times** across all three translated volumes, against 593 occurrences of *Taiyayé*. The anecdote is not there. Printed as a FIX box rather than quietly dropped.
2. **Shahîd's foundations — withdrawn as unverified.** The claim that "Ghassān post Ghassān" rests entirely on 11th–14th-century Arabic literature could not be checked; BASIC is not obtainable. Named as an outstanding item instead.
3. **"Mujīr al-Dīn mentions Ghassān zero times" → once, formulaically.** One hit in each of two editions: al-Ḥārith al-Ghassānī in the list of the Prophet's letters to kings. Falsifiable in five seconds; not shipped.
4. **"The Karak Ḥaddādīn said they came from *b. el-Ḥaddād*, a lineage name" → *ḫ.* not *b.*** Musil's abbreviation for *ḫirbe*. The page was rendered at 500 dpi to settle it. **The correction strengthens the finding** — a lineage name is a claim about ancestry; a ruin is a claim about ground.
5. **"*Ṣalība* means those who passed out of Arab status" — inverted.** It means pure, unmixed descent. The passing-out is the predicate al-ʿUmarī applies to them, not the definition. Corrected in the citation, and the corrected reading is the stronger one.

And four numbers fixed: **J-Z640 is 4,600 ybp, not 3,600**; **Nebel 2000 is in *Human Genetics*, not AJHG**; **Zalloua 2024 states no generation interval** (Haber 2017, 28 years, is the defensible version); **Fernandes 2015 is mtDNA** and has been dropped from the male-line argument.
