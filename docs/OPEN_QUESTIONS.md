# Open questions

Ordered by how much they would change the book.

## 1. Ankara — the eighty names of 1596

A *mufaṣṣal* register names every household head with his father's name. The
1596–97 defter of the nāḥiya of al-Quds therefore **names all eighty heads of
household at Ramallah**. What has been published is Hütteroth and Abdulfattah's
*tabulation* — counts, not names. Nobody has asked for the folio.

Where: Tapu ve Kadastro Genel Müdürlüğü, Ankara.

## 2. Istanbul — is it حديد or حداد?

Registers **I.S. 289** (Bayt Jālā, 961 AH / 1553–54) and **I.S. 516** (Ramallah,
970 AH / 1562–63), in the Arabic, read for the pointing.

*Jareer bin Hadeed* appears in **both** registers and is one of the nineteen
households that make the move. حديد ("iron") and حداد ("blacksmith") are one
letter apart and in an Ottoman clerk's hand barely distinguishable. If the second
reading is right, the family name is in a document nine years before the town
exists.

This is exactly the kind of surname resemblance the book warns against elsewhere,
and it is printed as a lead with the trap named beside it.

## 3. The Karak clan book of 1991

Does **Ṣaqr**, and the date **1561**, stand in عشائر الحدادين حتى عام ١٩٩١م
(Khalaf Khalīl, Munīr Jiryis, ʿAwwād Jiryis Ḥaddādīn)?

Less urgent than it was: Ḥammūdeh prints *Rāshid bin Ṣaqir al-Ḥaddādīn* in 2014
on the authority of Khalīl Abū Rayya (1980), so the tradition is in Arabic print
by 1980 at the latest.

Where: the Ḥaddādīn diwan in Amman, or the Jordanian National Library deposit
record.

## 4. Sijill 48, p. 54

The bibliography appendix reports a boundary dispute over قرية رام الله at p. 54
of Jerusalem register 48, 23 Dhū al-Qaʿda 972 / 22 June 1565. **That reading comes
from a machine decode of the IRCICA scan and has not been checked against the page
images.** It is not the waqf entry (which is p. 88, 29 June 1565) and should not
be cited until somebody reads the folio.

## 5. Things the book should be telling through Ramallah's people and is not

- **Tell es-Sultan.** The excavation running since 1997 is Palestinian — Hamdan
  Taha of the Department of Antiquities with Lorenzo Nigro of Rome «La Sapienza» —
  and it wrote the file on which UNESCO inscribed the site in 2023. The book still
  tells Jericho through Kenyon and Bar-Yosef.
- **The terraces.** Al-Houdalieh and Ghadban counted **167 agricultural
  watchtowers over some three thousand dunums** in al-Tireh quarter and ʿAin Qinia
  village (*International Journal of Architectural Heritage* 7:5, 2013). That is
  the district's own oldest surviving technology, still in use, and it is not in
  the book.
- **Amarna.** EA 365: Biridiya of Megiddo complaining that he alone provides the
  corvée gangs. The letters are a file about who works the fields of this country,
  and the people in the fields are the ancestors of the people in these villages.

## 6. Smaller

- **St Andrew's, 1887** — carried by Shāhīn alone; no diocesan record found.
- **PCBS 2024 projection** — cite the exact table, or print 42,122 (2021).
- **The Hourani study** on the Maalouf forgery is unrefereed and self-posted; the
  book leans on it for its comparative standard and should say so where it uses it.
- **The eighth bachelor.** John's 1562 transcription marks seven; the register's
  own total is eight.
