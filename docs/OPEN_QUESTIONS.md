# Worked on 10 October 2026

  from the book.
- **The 1186 charter** does not pledge Ramallah itself. It pledges an unnamed hamlet
  near the road to Ramallah (Strehlke p. 18, no. 20). The book is corrected.
- **The date of the 1953 notebook** is 8 August 1953. Shāhīn's English section says
  so; his Arabic writes 4/8/1953.

IX/1, has nothing of the kind, and his "600 Shawbak Christians" in Barqūq's army is a
report he himself calls false. The error appears to come from Milwright.

# CLOSED on 7 September 2026

**C1. Jifnā 1834 — closed, and it was a correction.** Robinson & Smith, *Biblical
Researches* (1841), III.78–79, read directly: *"After the rebellion of 1834,
twenty-six men were taken to Egypt and put into the public works, whence they have
never returned. Not long since, one of their two priests went to Egypt to look
after them, and died there."* The received version — 26 exiled *for their alleged
participation*, and *two priests joining voluntarily* — is a misparse of "one of
their two priests," and it is what the general reference works print. The book now
quotes Robinson and prints the correction in a FIX box. **Cite the 1841 edition:
the 1856 second edition renumbers the volumes.**

**C2. Why Jifnā was punished — closed.** Not a local rising. Jifnā belonged to the
**northern Banī Ḥārith**, whose paramount shaykh **Ismāʿīl Simḥān was executed by
Ibrāhīm Pasha in 1834**. Macalister & Masterman, *PEF QS* 37 (1905), p. 354.

**C3. The Samḥān nāḥiya — closed, and there was never a conflict.** Banī Ḥārith
was **bipartite**: the *northern* half under **Simḥān**, seats al-Jāniya and
**Rās Karkar** ("er-Rās… also called Rās Karker" — PEF's own footnote), villages
including Jifnā, Bīr Zayt, Dayr ʿAmmār, Surdah; the *southern* half under the
**Kaʿraja** at Bayt Ibzīʿ. **Banī Zayd is separate**, the Barghūthīs' own, seated
at Dayr Ghassāna. The circulating table collapses the two halves and parks Rās
Karkar in Banī Zayd, **citing Doumani p. 35 — where no such table exists**;
Doumani's only nāḥiya table is Jabal Nablus 1850 and contains none of these names.
**Drop that citation wherever it appears.** Corroborated by the Institute for
Palestine Studies study of the Abū Ghūsh family.

**C4. Which nāḥiya Ramallah was in — closed.** **Jabal al-Quds**, in the quarter
administered from al-Bīra, listed among "the other villages of this district" and
**not as a seat**. PEF *QS* 37 (1905), pp. 353–54. The book's claim that Ramallah
was not a throne village is now positively sourced, not an argument from silence.

**C5. Rood's chronology — closed at the article level.** Judith Mendelsohn Rood,
"The Time the Peasants Entered Jerusalem," *Jerusalem Quarterly* **27** (Summer
2006), **28–43**, DOI 10.70190/jq.i27.p28 — read directly. Confirms verbatim: the
conscription order of 25 April 1834 (one in five); the abandoned sewer near the
Dung Gate, pointed out by the people of **Silwan**, 36 men through it on 20 May;
Ibrāhīm's 9,000 troops and 1,500 losses. Her own evidence is the Jerusalem Islamic
court records and the chronicle of the Greek Orthodox monk **Spyridon**, pp. 88–92.

**C6. Bayt Jālā, 1834 — closed, and it is now in the book.** **31 May**, the day
after Ibrāhīm re-entered Jerusalem: soldiers attacked Bayt Jālā and killed **33
Christian men and women**, on suspicion of **looting**. Rood, *JQ* 27.

**C7. The eighth bachelor — closed, and it turned out to be a good question.**
Ḥammūdeh reads the 1562 register as **63 households + 8 bachelors**; the family's
own transcription of the folio reads **64 + 7**. **Both total 71 Christian
entries.** The disagreement is a single line, read once as a household and once as
a bachelor. That is the eighth bachelor, and he is now in print as a conflict.

**C8. The AFRP's founding date — closed.** Three dates because three foundings.
**1952:** six Ramallah immigrants at Wayne State begin *Hāthihi Rām Allāh*.
**7 September 1959, Detroit:** the clubs resolve to constitute a Federation and
adopt interim by-laws; first convention that year. **August 1963:** IRS recognition
— AFRP, EIN 38-6096448, 501(c)(4); American Ramallah Federation Education Fund,
EIN 38-6096449, 501(c)(3); both at Westland, Michigan. The convention count settles
it arithmetically: the 50th fell in 2008 and the 63rd in 2022, and both close on
1959, not 1958. Shāhīn's "incorporated 1958" is best read as the clubs' coalition.
*The Ramallah Foundation of New York is older and separate: founded 1943,
incorporated in New York 1944 by its own account, IRS ruling February 1947.*

**C9. When Ramallah became Muslim-majority — closed as far as the record allows,
and the book's bracket was thirty years too late.** The 1967 census counted
**6,966 Christians of 12,134 — 57 per cent** — with 5,203 people (43%) originating
from what had become Israel. By 1997, 32 per cent. **The crossing happened between
1967 and 1997 and cannot be dated more closely: there was no census of any kind in
those thirty years**, and no municipal, church or scholarly source publishes a
locality-level religious breakdown for the interval. The 1967 figure is an
**Israeli state source**, produced by the occupying power weeks into the
occupation; the book says so on the page. Its tables are independently published
by the Levy Economics Institute of Bard College, so the reader can check them.

**C10. The PCBS projection — closed.** *Projected Mid-Year Population for Ramallah
& al-Bireh Governorate by Locality 2017–2021*, locality code **301810**: Ramallah
38,660 (2017) → 41,246 (2020) → **42,122 (2021)**. The 2017–2026 table is attested
by title but PCBS has retired the URL scheme; **no 2024 figure is printed, and none
was extrapolated.**

**C11. Al-Jalazūn's population — closed as a category error, not a conflict.**
UNRWA's own page gives "around 9,450" beside a density of 51,383/km² on 0.253 km²
— *and 51,383 × 0.253 = 13,000*. The density was computed from ~13,000 and printed
beside a stale headline. UNRWA counts **registered refugees**; PCBS counts
**residents** (8,201 in 2017). Both numbers are right about different things.

**C12. Khirbet et-Tireh — closed, and it corrected the book.** Al-Houdalieh's own
publication of the oil press says *"the press facility itself was maintained in
continuous use from the Byzantine through the late Ottoman periods."* **The book's
"abandoned after the earthquake of 749 and reoccupied only in the Ottoman period"
is withdrawn.** The press is a **direct-screw** installation with **four press-beds**
and a crushing basin 2.1 m across, on a settlement of ~30,000 m², with rooms above
to hold the owner's share — a communal press taking a cut, not a household one.
Salah Hussein al-Houdalieh, *Jerusalem Quarterly* 62 (2015), 84–96, free.

**C13. Riwaq's registry — closed, and it is free.** buildings.riwaq.org: 50,320
buildings in 422 localities, surveyed 1994–2007, no login. **Ramallah 381 historic
buildings** (the largest concentration in the governorate), **al-Bīra 168** — and
Ramallah's list is given **house by house under its owners' names**. The nearest
thing that exists to a house-by-house gazetteer of this town.

**C14. Little's *Catalogue* — closed by making it unnecessary.** McGill University
Library has put **the entire 1978 photographic survey of the Ḥaram corpus** on the
Internet Archive, open, no account: four reels, ~883 documents, verified as real
PDFs with an Arabic OCR layer. **The printed catalogue is the finding aid to a
corpus you can now read for free.** Also: **CALD** (cald.irht.cnrs.fr, IRHT/CNRS)
carries Jerusalem Ḥaram documents with full Arabic transcriptions and images;
registration appears free; it publishes 417 items total, so its Ḥaram coverage is
substantial but **partial**.

## 3. Newly opened by this sweep

- **The Rafeedie's origin is unsourced in Ḥammūdeh.** His endnote 9 on that
  sentence is an explanatory aside about the 1948 refugees, not a citation. The
  nearest reference is his note 8, to **al-Dabbāgh, *Bilāduna Filasṭīn*, vol. 8
  pt. 2, p. 258** — which he does not attach to it. *Go to al-Dabbāgh and see
  whether the Rafīdiyā origin is there.* The book now prints the claim as
  Ḥammūdeh's assertion, not as a documented finding.
- **Bethlehem's Muslim quarter, 1 June 1834 — withdrawn pending a page.** The
  claim circulates sourced to Rood 2004, p. 130. Her *article* says something
  else for that date. Needs Rood 2004, pp. 129–130, read directly.
- **Rood 2004's pagination** (pp. 123–34) is still second-hand; the events are
  confirmed from her article, the page numbers are not.
- **The *Shuʾūn Filasṭīniyya* page range** for Ḥammūdeh's al-Bīra article.
  Issue **275–276 (2019)** confirmed; pagination needs the print issue.
- **Al-Jalazūn's lease.** ARIJ, Bshara and OCHA describe it three ways that are
  probably one arrangement — but **no source produces the instrument, its date or
  its parties.** Adel Yahya, *A Story of a Camp: al-Jalazone* (PACE, 2006), is the

- **Khirbat al-Tira's churches** are published separately — *Journal of Eastern

## 4. Carried forward, unchanged

- **Is it حديد or حداد?** Registers I.S. 289 and I.S. 516 read for the pointing.
  *Jareer bin Hadeed* is in both and is one of the nineteen who move. One letter.
- **The Karak clan book of 1991.** Confirmed to exist, and a 2011 *al-Raʾy*
  feature quotes it: the name from land-demarcation in the **Shawbak and Karak**
  region, with an alternative tradition tying it to **al-Ḥudayda in Yemen**; and
  a migration in which **Ṣabra** divided his inheritance among five sons and a
  daughter, three sons refused and left Karak, and the move to Māʿīn began **1894**
  in successive drought years. *That is a newspaper's paraphrase of a
  family-produced book, not the book.* **Beware a decoy:** a different عشائر
  الحدادين by عباس حدادين was launched in Amman.
- **Sijill 48, p. 54** — the boundary dispute reading comes from a machine decode
  of the IRCICA scan and has not been checked against the page images. Not the
  waqf entry (p. 88). Do not cite until somebody reads the folio.
- **St Andrew's, 1887** — Shāhīn alone; no diocesan record found.
- **The Hourani study** on the Maalouf forgery is unrefereed and self-posted; say
  so where the book leans on it.
- **Ramallah municipality records before 1948** — never consulted.

## 5. Gaps identified and NOT filled

Ranked by size. Each has a named source, several already on the shelf and uncited.

- **The Mandate as a political period** — no 1936, no Great Revolt, no partition.
  Rashid Khalidi, *The Iron Cage*; Khalil Totah's diaries; the 1,132 digitised
  issues of *Mirʾāt al-Sharq* the book calls unexploited and leaves unexploited.
- **The economic base** — "soap" appears nowhere in a book about a Palestinian
  hill town. Olive oil, the Nablus soap industry, remittance capital replacing
  agricultural capital. Doumani (now on the shelf); Seikaly, *Men of Capital*.
  *The Khirbat al-Tira press, four beds and an owner's share, is the local end of
  this and is now in hand.*
- **Women** — 66 generations, all male, and the book never remarks on it. The
  marriage-ring practice is described as a church mechanism rather than as how
  women's marriages built the clan structure. Fleischmann, *The Nation and Its
  "New" Women*; Moors, *Women, Property and Islam*.
- **Church institutions** — the Arab-versus-Greek clergy struggle, which is the
  political history of Ramallah's own Orthodox majority, is absent.
- **1967 and after** — one good paragraph. Taraki (on the shelf); Jubeh, cited six
  times in the bibliography and never in the narrative.
- **The Mamluk and early Ottoman centuries** — Zeʾevi, *An Ottoman Century*, on
  the shelf, cited once in a note, never used.
